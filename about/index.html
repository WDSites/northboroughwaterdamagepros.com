Northborough Water Damage Pros answers calls about water damage and mold in Northborough, Massachusetts and the nearby towns of Westborough, Marlborough, Shrewsbury, Southborough and Berlin. The line is open 24 hours a day. The dispatcher asks about the source, the rooms and the risks, then arranges licensed and insured restoration professionals.

We keep this page short because we would rather show you how the work is done than tell you how good we are. You will find that on each service page.

How we work

Every job starts with safety. The dispatcher asks about electrical risk, gas smells and the type of water, because those answers change the first steps. Moisture meters and a thermal camera map the damage, extraction and drying equipment goes in, and readings confirm when the structure is dry.

The scope and pricing are explained before work starts. If the scope changes because walls are opened and more damage is found, that conversation happens before the work continues.

Standards we reference

The restoration industry follows two published standards. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold remediation. We name them because you can ask any company, including us, whether its crews work to them. We do not claim any firm certification on this site.

What we do not do

We do not do basement waterproofing, foundation repair or plumbing repair. When a recurring leak needs one of those trades, we tell you so. We do not handle fire and smoke restoration on this site. We do not promise a claim outcome, a billing arrangement with your insurer or an arrival time.

A job from first call to final reading

  1. 1

    Call and describe

    The dispatcher records the address, the water source and any safety risk.

  2. 2

    Stop the source

    A crew or you shut off the supply, because nothing dries while water is still arriving.

  3. 3

    Remove water

    Pumps and extraction units take out standing water and soaked material.

  4. 4

    Map and dry

    Moisture meters and a thermal camera show where water traveled, and drying equipment goes where the readings say.

  5. 5

    Confirm dry

    Readings are taken again until the structure matches a dry reference, and the job is not called done before that.

Rebuilding is a separate step. Drywall, flooring and paint go back after the readings are dry, and our water damage repair page explains that stage.

Questions worth asking any restoration company

  • Will you take moisture readings, and will I see them when the job ends?
  • What is included in the quote, and what could change the scope?
  • Are the people who work in my home licensed and insured?
  • How do you treat contaminated water, and what protective gear do you use?
  • Who do I call if something looks wrong after you leave?

A company that answers these plainly is easier to trust than one that leads with promises. Ask the same questions of everyone you call, including us, and keep the answers with your other records.

What to do before anyone arrives

  • Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main, if you can do it safely
  • Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water
  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything
  • Lift small items, pillows and paper off wet floors, and leave heavy furniture alone
  • Keep children and pets out of wet rooms, and never use a household vacuum on water

These steps cost nothing and they protect both the building and your claim. Our water mitigation page explains what the crew does next, and the emergency water removal page covers the first hour in more detail.

How we handle insurance questions

Your insurer decides what your policy covers, and we cannot speak for it. What we can do is describe the work in plain terms, keep records of the readings, and tell you which photos and receipts to save. Call your agent early, ask what the policy says about sudden discharge, sewer backup and flood, and keep every document in one folder.

We do not promise a claim outcome, and we do not tell you what your carrier will pay. If a company does, ask it to put that promise in writing.
