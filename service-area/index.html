Towns we cover

Westborough (ZIP code 01581) is the Route 9 town with the Sudbury and Assabet headwaters and Lake Chauncy. Marlborough (ZIP code 01752) is a Middlesex County city with multi-unit buildings and I-495 offices. Shrewsbury (ZIP code 01545) is a town of streetcar-era houses along Lake Quinsigamond.

Southborough (ZIP codes 01772 and 01745) has the Sudbury Reservoir and its villages, and Berlin (ZIP code 01503) is a rural town between the Nashua and Assabet River valleys.

Nearby places we also take calls from

Calls from Hudson (ZIP code 01749), Grafton (ZIP code 01519), Clinton (ZIP code 01510) and Worcester (ZIP codes 01602 through 01610) are taken when crews are available. These towns do not have their own pages here.

What changes from town to town

The services are the same everywhere, but the typical call is different. A lakeshore house and a downtown condo fail differently, and a farmhouse with an outbuilding adds access questions. Reading your town's page tells you what dispatch will ask about, and what you can do before a crew arrives.

The service pages explain what each step involves: emergency water removal, water mitigation, flood damage restoration and the rest.

What to have ready when you call

  • The street address and the nearest cross street, which matters most on rural roads and on streets that change names at a town line
  • Where the water started and whether it has been stopped at a valve or at the main
  • Which floors and rooms are wet, and about how long the water has been there
  • Whether the water touches an outlet, a panel, a furnace or an appliance, and whether you smell gas
  • Whether the building is a house, a rented unit or a commercial space, and who owns it

These answers let the dispatcher line up the right equipment before anyone leaves. A tenant call, for instance, also needs the landlord's name, and a commercial call needs the unit number and the building contact.

Why the town matters for your claim and your permits

Permits and inspections are handled by each town's own departments, so a repair that needs a permit in one town follows that town's process. Marlborough is in Middlesex County while the other towns on this list are in Worcester County, and the county line changes some of the offices you will deal with. Your town page names what we can confirm from public sources and leaves out what we cannot.

Insurance works the same way in every town. Take photos before anything is moved, keep receipts and read your policy for the wording on sudden discharge, sewer backup and flood. Those notes help whichever town you live in.

Questions the dispatcher may ask

Expect a few direct questions. The dispatcher will ask whether the water is clean, gray or from a sewer, because the answer decides the protective gear and what can be saved. It will ask whether anyone has been hurt, whether the electrical system is involved, and whether the building has been lived in recently. A vacant house or a closed-up outbuilding is handled differently from an occupied home.

None of this is a screening test. It is how the right people and tools get sent the first time, and it keeps you from paying for a second trip.

If your town is not on this list

Call anyway. The dispatcher will say whether a crew can reach you and how long it might take, and will not promise an arrival time that cannot be kept. If the water is rising, shut off the supply at the main valve and keep people away from electrical equipment while you wait.
