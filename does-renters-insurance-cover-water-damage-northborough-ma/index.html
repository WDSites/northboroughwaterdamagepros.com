Does Renters Insurance Cover Water Damage in Northborough, MA?

A plain-English look at what renters policies usually cover after a leak, and the steps that protect your claim.

☎ Call (508) 446-9244

By the Northborough Water Damage Pros team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

If you rent in Northborough, MA and water starts coming through the ceiling, the first question is usually who pays. A renters policy typically covers your belongings and some living costs after a sudden water event, but it does not cover the building. That belongs to your landlord's policy.

The details depend on your policy wording, so treat this page as a map for your questions to the insurer, not a promise of coverage. What follows explains the common patterns and the steps that keep your claim clean.

What a renters policy usually covers

Most renters policies cover personal property such as furniture, clothing and electronics when a covered event damages them. Water events that are sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe, an overflowing tub or a failed appliance hose, are the common examples.

Many policies also cover additional living expenses if the unit becomes unlivable, and personal liability if water from your unit damages a neighbor's. Read the declarations page for the limits and the deductible, because those numbers shape what you actually receive.

What it usually does not cover

Gradual leaks, long-term seepage and damage from neglect are commonly excluded. Flood is usually excluded as well, and sewer or drain backup often needs a separate endorsement. The walls, floors and fixtures of the building are the landlord's responsibility, not yours.

If a pipe in the wall of your apartment fails, the landlord's insurance generally handles the building and your policy handles your contents. If your own actions caused the leak, your liability coverage may respond. Ask both insurers instead of guessing.

The first hour: protect your claim

  1. 1

    Stop the water if you safely can

    Close the shutoff under the fixture or the unit shutoff. If you do not know where it is, ask the building manager now, not during a leak.

  2. 2

    Photograph before you move anything

    Take wide shots and close-ups of the water, the ceiling and each damaged item.

  3. 3

    Notify the landlord in writing

    A text or email creates a record with a time stamp.

  4. 4

    Call your insurer

    Ask what they need and what temporary repairs they expect you to make.

  5. 5

    Call a restoration company if contents or the unit need drying

    Tell the landlord who is working in the unit.

Keep receipts for anything you buy to limit the damage, such as a wet vacuum rental or towels. Do not throw away damaged items until the insurer says you can, and do not sign a repair contract until you understand who is paying.

Where restoration fits in

In a rental, the landlord usually chooses and pays for the building repairs, while you may be responsible for contents cleanup. A crew doing water mitigation will dry the structure, and water damage cleanup handles your belongings and any contaminated materials. If your building is in Marlborough or Westborough, the same line serves those towns.

Talking to your landlord: put it in writing

A phone call is fine for an emergency, but follow it with a written message the same day. State the date and time you noticed the water, where it is coming from if you know, which rooms are affected and what you have already done, such as closing a valve. Attach photographs.

Written notice protects you in two ways. It proves when the landlord knew, and it creates a paper trail if the repair drags on. Keep a copy of every message and note any reply, including who said what and when.

Ask the landlord in the same message who is arranging the drying and repairs, and whether you may let a restoration crew into the unit. A crew needs access to dry the structure, and the landlord or building manager is usually the one who authorizes it.

Common mistakes renters make after a leak

  • Throwing out damaged belongings before photographing them or telling the insurer
  • Waiting for the landlord to act before calling their own insurer
  • Assuming the building policy covers their furniture and clothing
  • Skipping a written notice because the landlord answered by phone
  • Running fans and heaters on a wet carpet without anyone checking the pad
  • Ignoring a musty smell that appears a few weeks after the leak

The last item matters more than it seems. A leak that was dried late can grow mold inside a wall within weeks, and the smell is often the first clue. If it appears, tell the landlord in writing and ask for the area to be inspected.

Northborough note: who to call about the building

If a leak or a damp wall in a rental is not being fixed, Massachusetts guidance says to contact the local board of health or inspectional services division to request an inspection. The Town of Northborough has a health department, and its website lists how to reach it.

It also helps to know where water enters the picture. Water in Northborough arrives from the MWRA system, according to the authority's community letter for 2022, and the town carries it to each address. A break in the street main is a town matter, while a failure inside your unit is the building owner's.

Questions Northborough Homeowners Ask

Who do I call in Northborough if my landlord will not fix a leak?

Start with written notice to the landlord. If nothing happens, Massachusetts guidance directs tenants to the local board of health or inspectional services division to request an inspection. In Northborough that means the town health department.

Does renters insurance cover mold after a leak?

It depends on the policy and the cause. Mold that follows a covered sudden leak may be partly covered, and mold from a long-standing problem often is not. Ask your insurer in writing.

Will my insurer pay for a restoration company I choose?

You can usually choose, but the insurer may have rules about documentation and approval. Call before signing anything, and ask what they will want to see.

Is flood from a storm covered under a renters policy?

Often not. Flood is commonly excluded and needs a separate policy, so read the exclusions in your own policy and ask about flood coverage if your unit is on a low floor.
