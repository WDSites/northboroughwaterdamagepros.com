When water is spreading in Marlborough

The urgent calls fall into three groups. The first is a pipe, tank or appliance failure in a multi-family or condo building, where water passes through ceilings into units below. The second is storm water entering a basement. The third is a commercial leak that stops a business from opening.

In each case, stop the source if you can reach it, call the line and tell dispatch how many units or rooms are affected. The sooner the crew knows the scope, the sooner they can plan extraction and drying.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

1

Find the shutoff

In a condo or apartment, the unit shutoff may be separate from the building main. Close whichever you can reach and tell the manager.

2

Protect the floor below

If water is leaking through a ceiling, place buckets and move belongings out of the affected rooms below.

3

Cut power safely

Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry. Water near light fixtures or outlets is a reason to call the utility.

4

Document and call

Photograph the water and the damage in each unit. Call the line and your landlord or association.

Why multi-unit water losses take more planning

Marlborough grew as a shoe-manufacturing city, with the industry starting in 1836 and shaping the older housing and the downtown buildings around Main Street. Many older buildings have been divided into apartments, which means a single drain line may serve several units.

When water crosses a unit boundary, drying has to be coordinated. The crew checks the unit below the leak, the wall cavities and the common areas, and documents the moisture in each unit. That documentation helps tenants, owners and insurers sort out responsibility.

Neighborhoods, roads and what they mean for response

Marlborough sits at a crossroads of I-495, I-290, Route 20 and Route 85. That is useful when a call comes from the Southwest Quadrant office parks, the downtown historic district or a house near Lake Williams. It also means traffic can slow a crew at rush hour, so no arrival time is promised.

The city has several lakes and reservoirs, including Lake Williams, Millham Reservoir and Fort Meadow Reservoir, and the Assabet River crosses its northwest corner. Homes near those water bodies can see seepage after heavy storms.
