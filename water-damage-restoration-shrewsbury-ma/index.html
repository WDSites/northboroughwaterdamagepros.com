Housing eras and the water problems that come with them

Single-family housing in Shrewsbury grew along the town's streetcar routes, and the population roughly doubled between 1915 and 1940. Houses from that period are now eighty years old or more. Their original drain lines, supply lines and basement walls may have been replaced, patched or left alone, and each one fails differently.

Newer houses fail in different places. Supply lines to appliances, ice maker lines and water heater connections cause most of the leaks in houses built in the last few decades. Older houses leak at foundation joints, old pipe fittings and flat roof edges.

When water is spreading in Shrewsbury

Call at once if water is running, if it has reached the electrical panel or outlets, if a basement has taken in more than an inch or if sewage is backing up. Hilly terrain cut by small streams means storm runoff can find its way to low basements on the same street where a neighbor stays dry.

If water is still spreading, do this first

1

Close the main

Find the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the pipe enters the basement, and close it fully.

2

Mind the panel

Shut off power only from dry footing. If the panel is in a wet basement, stay out and call the utility.

3

Move contents up

Lift boxes, rugs, electronics and paper off the floor, especially in a basement.

4

Call and describe

Tell dispatch the source, the rooms affected and whether the water came from a drain.

Around Lake Quinsigamond and the parks

Lake Quinsigamond runs along Shrewsbury's western edge. Its lakeside cottages, many converted to year-round houses, are small, close to the water and often built on slab or shallow crawl space foundations that dry slowly after a flood. Streets near Prospect Park, Jordan Pond and Dean Park have more trees and older drainage.

Lakeside owners should look up their address on the FEMA flood map and ask the insurer about flood and sewer backup wording, since standard Massachusetts home policies leave flood out.

Basements, sump pumps and storms

Shrewsbury's hilly terrain sends runoff downhill, and basements at the bottom of a slope take in the most. A sump pump that fails during a storm is a common cause of a call, and so is a window well that fills.

Test the pump each spring by pouring water into the pit, and consider a battery backup for storms that knock out power. If water has already entered, dispatch can arrange extraction and drying, and the flood damage restoration page explains the categories.

What to do before a crew arrives in Shrewsbury

  • Stop the water at the nearest shutoff and do not step into standing water near outlets
  • Do not use a household vacuum or a wet floor fan plugged in over wet ground
  • Move small valuables and papers to a dry room, and photograph everything first
  • Keep the garage and basement doors clear for the crew's hoses and equipment

My house was built in the 1920s. Does that change how drying is done?

Yes. Plaster walls, wood trim and old wood floors dry and fail differently from modern drywall. Tell dispatch the age of the house, and the crew will plan for it.

Does home insurance cover a flooded basement near the lake?

Standard Massachusetts home policies leave flood out, so a lakeside basement needs a separate flood policy or endorsement to be covered. Read your own policy and call your agent.
